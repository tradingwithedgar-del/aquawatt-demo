01 — The orange one

Thambili, not the green kind.

King coconut is its own variety, native to Sri Lanka and known there as thambili. Bright orange husk, shorter tree, and a water that comes out subtly sweet on its own.

It is what people actually drink there, sold by the roadside and opened in front of you.

02 — The ingredient list

One line long.

King coconut water. That is the whole list. No added sugar, no preservatives, no concentrate reconstituted somewhere else.

If a coconut water needs a flavour system to taste good, it started out tasting like nothing.

Single ingredient
03 — The bottle

Glass, on purpose.

Plastic and lined cartons are cheaper to ship and easier to stack. We went with glass so the water is not sitting against plastic between Sri Lanka and your fridge, and so nothing leaches into it on the way.

350ml, gold cap, made to be drunk cold.

04 — The source

The Coconut Triangle.

Sourced from the belt of coastal plain and plantation between Puttalam, Kurunegala and Gampaha, in Sri Lanka's north west. It grows more than half the country's coconuts.

Sourced, pasteurised and bottled there, so it leaves the island already finished.
